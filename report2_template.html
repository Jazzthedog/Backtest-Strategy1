Read this first · Not a like-for-like comparison

These two things do not carry the same risk. Buy & hold is 100% invested every single day. The strategy holds a 10% position and is in the market only of the time — an average net exposure of about , roughly less market risk. A large part of the return gap is simply that difference, not a failure of the signal.

That is why the static 10% / 90% cash control is carried through every panel below. It holds the identical exposure to the strategy and makes no timing decisions, so it isolates the signal itself. The strategy loses to that control too ( against ), which is the finding that actually matters.

Net exposure = position size × share of days holding it; the average fraction of your money actually at risk in the market.

Year by year — when did the strategy actually help?

The one genuine strength

WHAT THIS SHOWS — Calendar-year returns side by side, revealing that the strategy's value was concentrated entirely in falling markets.

Each year is measured from the previous year-end balance; the first year is measured from the $100,000 starting balance.

Genuine finding

In the six calendar years QQQ fell, the strategy beat the do-nothing control in five of them. In 2022 it lost 1.29% while the control lost 14.19% and buy & hold lost 31.35%. The exit rule does genuinely get out of the way of sustained declines.

But the cost was ruinous

It beat the control in only of 28 years. In strong bull years it gave up 5 to 16 percentage points at a stretch — 2023 alone cost 16.2 points against the control. Twenty-two years of lagging buys six years of cushioning.

How to read this honestly

A rule that only pays in falling markets is an insurance policy, not a growth engine. Judged as insurance the question is whether the premium — 22 years of underperformance — was worth the payout. Over this history, measured against the control, it was not.

Did the chosen start date rig the comparison?

No — checked

WHAT THIS SHOWS — A deliberate check that the benchmark's advantage is not an artifact of which day it was started on.

The strategy cannot trade until it has 20 bars of history, so its first possible trade is , not the first day of data. Buy & hold was therefore started on that same day rather than on the first bar available — the choice that is fairest to the strategy.

Starting buy & hold on the very first bar instead would have made it look better. The alignment used throughout this report understates the benchmark, so the gap reported here is a conservative one.

Method & provenance

WHAT THIS SHOWS — Exactly how to reproduce every number on this page.

Buy & hold construction

$100,000 into QQQ at the close of the strategy's first tradeable bar, held to the final bar. No rebalancing, no additions, no withdrawals. Dividends credited as cash on the ex-date, not reinvested — identical treatment to the strategy.

Control construction

$10,000 into QQQ on the same day (10% of the balance), $90,000 held as cash at 0%. Never traded again. Carries the strategy's exposure without any timing decision.

Prices

Yahoo raw OHLC with auto_adjust=False — split-adjusted, not dividend-adjusted, so all four fields share one scale. Verified: largest single-day close ratio in 27 years is 1.168×, with no ≈2.0× split gap.
